Validation

How good is this toy Monte Carlo?

Cascade's hadronic physics is a small calibrated toy, not QGSJET, EPOS or SIBYLL. This page puts a fixed, precomputed ensemble of vertical showers next to the analytic scalings it should follow and the full simulations it imitates, and says plainly where it falls short. Nothing is simulated when the page loads: every number comes from src/data/validation.json, written by scripts/precompute-validation.ts.

Honest limitations

  • Toy hadronic model, calibrated once. Energy-dependent p–air and π–air cross sections, a UA5-style multiplicity scaled to an air target, leading-particle inelasticity and exponential pT. Its one free multiplicity factor was tuned so that mean proton Xmax at 1 PeV lands near 555 g/cm². Agreement at 1 PeV is therefore by construction; agreement elsewhere, the elongation rate, the fluctuations and the muon numbers are the actual tests.
  • Missing particle production. No kaons, charm, baryon–antibaryon pairs, resonances or diffraction. Only pions are produced, so muon numbers and their energy dependence carry model uncertainty far larger than the statistical errors shown here.
  • No photonuclear interactions. Photons only pair-produce and Compton-scatter, so a gamma-ray shower here makes no hadrons and essentially no muons; real photon showers carry a few to ten per cent of the hadronic muon number.
  • No LPM effect and no photon pre-shower in the geomagnetic field. Both matter mainly above ~1018 eV, past the top of the photon ladder here, where real photon showers become deeper and fluctuate far more than a Bethe–Heitler cascade.
  • Nuclei by semi-superposition. Wounded nucleons interact; the spectator remnant breaks into one fragment plus free nucleons. No nuclear-fragmentation data enter.
  • Flat atmosphere, flat ground. Linsley's US-standard layers, no curvature, ground a plane at 1539 m (861 g/cm²). At the top of the ladder Xmax approaches the ground, so the Gaisser–Hillas fit has little or no tail to work with.
  • No geomagnetic field; it matters most for muons in inclined showers, which are not tested here.
  • Hillas thinning with a weight limit. Profiles and ground counts are weighted samples. Weighted counts are unbiased on average, but the thinning adds artificial fluctuations; the convergence check above tests the bias at one energy only.
  • Gaisser–Hillas fits on thinned profiles add noise to Xmax and Nmax, which inflates σ(Xmax) slightly. The charged profile counts e± above 1 MeV kinetic, muons and charged hadrons.
  • Small ensembles. 12–24 showers per point: the mean is good to a few g/cm², the spread to roughly 15–20 per cent, and a single rare deep proton can move both.
  • Everything is deterministic by seed. Rerunning the script reproduces the file exactly (except the CPU times). That makes the comparison repeatable, not more accurate.

References and method

  • Heitler–Matthews: J. Matthews, Astropart. Phys. 22 (2005) 387. Proton Xmax uses the code's approximate elongation form 470 + 58 log10(E/PeV); nuclei by superposition; photons X0 ln(E/ξc) with X0 = 36.62 g/cm², ξc = 84.8 MeV. Muons Nμ = A0.15(E/20 GeV)0.85. These are scalings with uncertain normalisation, not predictions to a few per cent.
  • Greisen electron number at the photon maximum, evaluated at the Heitler depth (code: greisen(E0, xmaxPhoton(E0))). It counts all electrons; the Monte Carlo counts charged particles above a 1 MeV kinetic cut.
  • Model band: mean Xmax of CORSIKA with EPOS-LHC, QGSJet-II-04 and Sibyll 2.3, approximate values digitised from published reviews (e.g. K.-H. Kampert & M. Unger, Astropart. Phys. 35 (2012) 660; Pierre Auger Collaboration Xmax papers), for orientation only. The reference widths σ(Xmax) ≈ 60–70 g/cm² (p) and 20–25 g/cm² (Fe) are typical full-simulation values from the same sources.
